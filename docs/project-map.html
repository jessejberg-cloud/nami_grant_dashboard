NAMI Dashboard Suite: the plan on one page

For NAMI Southeast Wisconsin leadership and Jesse Berg. Dated October 5, 2026. Three programs today, built to take more.

Week 1: emails go out
Weeks 2–4: staff test
Week 4: fixes
Decision: good spot?
Weeks 5–6: NAMI home
Week 7: move in

Weeks count from the day the emails go out. Phase two waits for one decision and one named person, so its dates move with those.

Phase one · now · testing

Try the programs on the links we have today

Nothing new to set up. Staff open the landing page and start. The goal is honest feedback: what is confusing, slow or missing.

Steps

  1. Jesse turns on the landing page and shares the two sign-in pages with each tester as an Editor.
  2. The Executive Director forwards each program's email to the person who will use it.
  3. Staff use the programs for real, small things: one grant, one event, a few leads. Example records are there to play with.
  4. Staff send feedback by replying to the email. Nothing is too small.
  5. Jesse fixes and improves as feedback comes in. Changes reach the links within minutes.
  6. NAMI names one internal person to own the accounts in phase two (see "Who does what").

Where things live in phase one

  • The code: Jesse's GitHub, open to read, with the guides.
  • The running programs: the Grant Dashboard on its current site; Grant Radar on the landing page, no sign-in, each person's own list; Volunteers & Events on a shared page with a Claude sign-in, which is what keeps the volunteer list private.
  • The rule for all three: no health details, background-check reports, ID numbers or bank details.
Phase two · after the decision · NAMI's own home

Move the programs into a place NAMI owns, with one sign-in

Same programs, same guides. What changes is who owns the accounts, where the records live, and how people get in: one sign-in for all three, by a code sent to the person's NAMI email, with Microsoft sign-in possible later.

Steps

  1. NAMI creates a GitHub organization and a Cloudflare account (both free at this size), with two owners: the internal person and Jesse.
  2. Jesse moves the code into the NAMI organization. History and guides come with it.
  3. Jesse builds the shared record store for Volunteers & Events on the NAMI account (about a day), and moves Grant Radar and the Grant Dashboard across.
  4. Jesse turns on the sign-in in front of all three and adds each tester's email.
  5. Jesse updates every link and guide to the new address and sends a fresh set of emails. The old landing page points to the new one.
  6. Staff bring their phase-one records across with the backup files each program already makes.
  7. NAMI decides later whether to connect Microsoft sign-in; no change to the programs is needed for that.

What phase two makes possible

  • New programs slot in behind the same door and the same sign-in.
  • A program with private information, such as money, can be limited to named people.
  • A paid tier, if it is ever needed, is a small monthly fee on the NAMI account, not a rebuild.
The decision between the phases. After about four weeks of testing: are the programs useful enough to make NAMI's own? If yes, phase two starts as soon as the internal person is named. If not yet, phase one continues with fixes, and the question is asked again.

Who does what

Jesse BergBuilds and edits the programs, keeps the guides current, moves everything in phase two. One of the two people able to change the code.
One internal NAMI personOwns the NAMI accounts alongside Jesse, decides who gets access, and is the second person able to change the code. Named by NAMI during phase one.
Staff who testUse the programs for real work, say what is confusing, and keep private details out. No setup beyond the email they are sent.

Later, when it is wanted

  • More programs. The landing page is built to take new cards. Every program follows the same rules: one name is enough, nothing is lost, every delete has Undo, plain words, and a Help page.
  • Private programs. A money dashboard, or anything with sensitive information, gets the locked door from phase two and its own list of named people. The more private the information, the smaller the list.
  • Connections. Reminders by email, documents on SharePoint, and Microsoft sign-in are all possible on the NAMI home, and are each a separate, later decision.
